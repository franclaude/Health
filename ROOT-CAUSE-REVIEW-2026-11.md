## 6. Re-reading specific tests

### The treadmill test of 2 February 2022

You asked that this not be misread, and the breath-by-breath record supports you.

- It was a Bruce protocol: 2.7 km/h at 10% gradient for 3 minutes, then 4.0 km/h at 12%, stopped at 5 min 20 s for breathlessness. You were on ivabradine 7.5 mg twice daily. Peak heart rate was 131, 67% of predicted. It was a short, submaximal test.
- Between minutes 3 and 4.7 your breathing rate fell to 8.6–11.7 breaths a minute, with breaths of about 2 litres and single breaths of 3.29 litres.
- End-tidal CO2 rose in exactly that period, to 6.5 kPa, and peaked at 7.0 kPa on another 3.28-litre breath at 10 breaths a minute.
- That is slow, very deep breathing with holds. It matches your description: needing a huge inhale for relief, and breath-holding as the only comfortable state.
- A 3.3-litre breath is two thirds of your vital capacity. Weak breathing muscles cannot do that, so the letter's "muscle weakness … to exclude" is answered by the test itself.
- The resting "arterial" CO2 of 6.20 kPa was recorded while the breath reading was 4.58 kPa. A gap of 1.6 kPa is not physiological with normal lungs (normal is about 0.2–0.7). The likeliest explanations are that the two were not taken at the same moment or a sample-handling error. The end-of-exercise pair (5.66 and 5.93) is coherent.
- The published studies of post-COVID illness, ME/CFS and orthostatic tachycardia consistently report low CO2 from over-breathing. A targeted search found no series reporting high CO2. Your pattern is unusual, and the test shows it came from how you were breathing.

### The raised base excess

- Three samples over 20 months show base excess of +3 to +6 and bicarbonate of 26–28.
- In April 2021 this was present while you were over-breathing (pH 7.50, CO2 4.70). That combination reads as a mild metabolic alkalosis, not as compensation for chronic CO2 retention.

## 8. So what is the immune change, and how could it actually be shown?

What the evidence supports:

- You were EBV-naive until about December 2016. Nothing immunological is demonstrable before that.
- Since January 2017 you carry latent EBV, as about 95% of adults do.
- Since then, and far more since 2020, infections trigger a stereotyped response: stiffness of neck and back, then mouth ulcers, then the chest symptoms, with incomplete recovery.
- Between flares, and on the dates that happened to be sampled, the measurable immune system is normal.

Where the science stands (2024–2026), so that you know what is and is not established:

| Proposed mechanism | Best human evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Persisting viral antigen | Antigen found in 43% with core symptoms against 21% without (Swank 2024) | Replicated but non-specific; antiviral trials negative |
| Complement activation | Two cohorts; discriminates modestly (Cervia-Hasler 2024; Baillie 2024) | Direction replicated; no treatment trial |
| Autoantibodies | Patient antibodies transferred to mice produce pain behaviour in three laboratories | Replicated for pain only; antibody-removal trials negative |
| EBV reactivation | Antibody footprints in four cohorts; virus not found in blood | Association only |
| Low blood return to the heart, poor oxygen extraction | Invasive exercise studies from one research network | Not independently replicated |
| Small-fibre nerve damage | 10 of 16 biopsies abnormal in one referral series | Uncontrolled |
| Low cortisol, low serotonin, brain inflammation on PET | Initial reports | Not reproduced or inconsistent |
| Persistent interferon excess | Raised at 8 months, largely resolved by 24 months | No evidence of persistence beyond that |

A central (brain-stem or hypothalamic) disorder of the "sickness response" is a reasonable hypothesis for chills without fever. The one small study of core temperature found it normal, and the measurable abnormality in adolescents was in sympathetic control of skin blood vessels (Wyller 2007). It remains a hypothesis with no validated test.

**The experiment that tests your hypothesis.** Your central claim is that your immune system over-reacts to viruses. Every blood test you have had was timed by appointment, not by flare. A paired sample, one within 48 hours of the first stiffness or ulcers and one in a settled week, would test the claim directly:

- CRP and serum amyloid A (an interleukin-1/6 type response shows here).
- Full blood count with differential, ferritin, CK.
- An interferon read-out. Interferon-driven responses suppress CRP, so a normal CRP does not exclude them. An accredited flow-cytometry marker (SIGLEC1/CD169 on monocytes) exists in at least one European laboratory; an interferon gene score is available in specialist centres.
- Complement activation products if a centre offers them.
- A swab of a fresh mouth ulcer for herpes simplex PCR, and a clinician or dated photograph to document the ulcers.

A flat result on all of these during a typical flare would be strong evidence that the abnormality is in how the response is regulated and felt, not in immune over-production. A positive result would be the first objective immune finding in ten years and would point to a specific pathway.

## 10. Findings that are probably separate from the main illness

### ALT

- The pattern is ALT above AST, normal GGT and ALP, normal ultrasound, with low HDL, sex-hormone-binding globulin of 13 nmol/L, ferritin up to 462 with normal iron saturation, and mildly raised IgA.
- That cluster is most economically explained by fat in the liver in a lean person. Ultrasound misses mild fat. IgA is raised in 46% of biopsied fatty liver (McPherson 2014).
- Exposures line up with several peaks: lymecycline (January–May 2017), herbal medicine (January 2019), a statin-based protocol (June 2022, 113), quetiapine (2026). Amitriptyline alone does not explain it, because ALT was 38–42 on amitriptyline in late 2017.
- Plotting ALT against the start and stop dates of every drug and supplement costs nothing.

### LDL cholesterol

- LDL of 2.7 at age 23 makes inherited high cholesterol very unlikely, because that condition is lifelong.
- A doubling over nine years in a lean man points to diet (low-carbohydrate and ketogenic diets raise LDL by about 1.9 mmol/L on average), inactivity and medication.

### IgE and IgA

- IgE of 753 with rhinitis indicates allergy. It is well below the level (over 2,000 with eczema and recurrent staphylococcal infection) that prompts a search for an inherited immune disorder.
- IgA of 3.3–3.9 is a mild rise that some laboratories would report as normal. It was last measured in May 2021.

### Low phosphate, December 2020

Phosphate was 0.73 during the weeks you were over-breathing; over-breathing drives phosphate into cells. It was normal afterwards.
